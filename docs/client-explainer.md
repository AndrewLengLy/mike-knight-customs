# What Your Website Can Now Tell Us

For Mike Knight. From Parabox Digital. August 28, 2026.

---

## The short version

Until now, your website could not tell you anything. People visited it, some of
them called, and the only real signal was whether the shop felt busy that week.

That is now fixed. From this month the site counts what actually happens on it:
how many people came, where they came from, how many asked for a quote, and how
many tapped your phone number. You get those numbers every month in a form that
takes two minutes to read.

**This does not mean you will get more leads next month.** It means we stop
guessing. When something is working we will be able to prove it and do more of
it. When something is wasting your time or money we will be able to see that
too, and stop it.

It also means that **if and when you decide to spend money on advertising, the
scoreboard is already built**. Every ad click can be traced forward: clicks to
visits, visits to quote requests, quote requests to a cost per lead. That is
the number that tells you whether advertising is worth it. Most shops start
advertising first and only later discover they cannot tell what it bought. You
are doing it the other way round, which is the right order.

## 1 · What we can see now that we could not see before

**Where people actually come from.** Not just "the internet". We can now tell a
person who clicked one of your ads from someone who found you on Google
naturally, from someone who came off your Yelp page, from someone who typed in
your address directly. Each one gets counted separately.

**Which link earned which quote request.** When somebody fills in the form, the
message that lands in your inbox now says where that person came from: a search,
your Yelp page, a link someone shared, or an ad once you run any. You can read
it yourself; you do not have to take my word for it.

**How many people tap your phone number.** Every phone number on the site is now
counted when someone taps it. Worth saying plainly: a tap is not a call. Some
people tap and change their mind. It is a useful signal, not a call log.

**Where people give up.** We can see how many people start filling in the quote
form and then stop, and which question they were on when they stopped. If a
field is putting people off, we will find it.

**Whether the form is broken.** This one sounds boring and is not. If quote
requests drop next month, the first question is whether fewer people wanted
one, or whether the form quietly stopped working. That used to take an
afternoon of poking around to answer. Now it is immediate.

**What people do on the page.** We can watch a recording of how a visit went:
where the mouse moved, what got clicked, how far down people read. It is how we
find the confusing parts. Anything a customer types into the form is blanked out
before it ever leaves their browser, so we never see names, numbers, or claim
details in those recordings.

## 2 · What that changes about where your money goes

You are not running Google Ads today. When you do, this is what the measurement
changes. Every ad used to look the same from the outside. Now they will not, so
we can make decisions instead of assumptions.

**Turn off what is not working.** If an ad brings visits but never a quote
request, it is costing money and returning nothing. We can now see that within
a month rather than a year, and stop it.

**Put more behind what is working.** If one ad is bringing in the vehicles you
actually want to work on, that is where the budget should go. Same for a
particular service or a particular town.

**Judge ads on quote requests, not clicks.** Clicks are cheap and easy to buy.
A shop full of the wrong work is not a win. Google's own bidding will be told
to aim at real quote requests, not at people tapping your phone number, because
otherwise it will happily buy you a pile of taps and call it success.

**Fix the site instead of buying more traffic.** Sometimes the problem is not
the ad, it is what happens after the click. If plenty of people arrive and
almost nobody asks for a quote, buying more visitors just wastes more money
faster. That is now something we can see rather than argue about.

## 3 · What you will get each month, and when

A short report, in your inbox, in the first week of each month, covering the
month before. It fits on one page and it is always the same shape, so it takes
about two minutes to read once you have seen one.

**Four numbers**

1. How many people visited the site.
2. How many asked for a quote.
3. What each quote request cost, once there is ad spend to divide by. Until
   then this line reads "no ad spend" rather than a made up number.
4. How many people tapped your phone number.

**Three actions.** The specific things I am doing about it this month. Not
suggestions, not options. What is being done.

**One recommendation.** The single thing I think is most worth your attention,
and what I need from you if anything. Usually nothing. Sometimes a decision
about budget, or a question only you can answer about the kind of work you want
more of.

If a number moves in a bad direction, it will be in the report. I would rather
tell you a number went the wrong way and what I am doing about it than quietly
leave it out.

## A note on privacy, because it is your name on the site

Measuring people means handling their information, so it has been done
properly rather than quietly.

- The website now has a plain-English privacy policy that names every tool we
  use and says exactly what each one collects.
- It says openly that visits get recorded, because they do, and burying that is
  how businesses get themselves in trouble.
- Anything a customer types into the quote form is blanked out of those
  recordings and never leaves their browser.
- Every page has a "Privacy Choices" link so a visitor can switch tracking off.
  If they do, the site works exactly the same.
- We do not sell anyone's information, and the policy says so.

One thing you should know: these pages are a solid, honest starting point, but
they are not attorney reviewed. Before you rely on them, it is worth having a
lawyer read them. I can tell you what they say and why. I cannot tell you they
are legally sufficient for your business, and I am not going to pretend
otherwise.

## What I need from you

Nothing, to start. The measurement is running.

Three things would help when you have a spare minute:

1. Tell me the kind of work you most want more of. The numbers can tell me what
   is happening; only you can tell me what a good month looks like.
2. Confirm whether you are running any Google ads yourself. I could not find an
   active ad account, and if one exists on your own Google login I need to know
   so its spend lands in the same report.
3. If you or the crew browse the site from the shop, that is fine, it is already
   filtered out so it does not skew your numbers.

Anything in the monthly report that does not make sense, ask. If a number needs
a paragraph of explanation from me before it means anything, it is the wrong
number and I will replace it.
