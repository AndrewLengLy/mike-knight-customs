Repair Protocol

Column C/A

Structural

Steel and aluminum returned to factory geometry, not "close enough," but measured against the numbers the manufacturer published.

Documentation That Gets OEM Approved

C/A·01

We send your insurance company the complete file: factory repair procedures, measurements, and part specifications. That is what gets OEM parts and a proper repair approved.

Deliverable: Factory Procedures · Measurements · OEM Approval

Multi-Panel Reconstruction

C/A·02

Replacement panels are joined exactly the way the factory joined them: correct weld type and count per the manufacturer's repair manual, seam sealer laid in the factory pattern, and every bare joint restored with e-coat-equivalent corrosion protection before refinish. Skipped steps show up about five winters later, as rust.

Weld Spec: State-of-the-Art Gyspot Welders & Riveters · Manufacturer Requirements

OEM Structural Parts Only

C/A·03

Crumple zones collapse in an engineered sequence that was validated in the manufacturer's crash-test program. Aftermarket rails, aprons, and reinforcements were not in that test, so they never go in your vehicle. If an insurer writes an imitation part into the estimate, we negotiate it back out before a wrench turns.

Parts Standard: Factory Part Numbers · No LKQ · No QRP

Column C/B

Electronic

Safety systems see the road through sensors mounted to the structure we just repaired. Verifying them is the second half of the repair.

Unibody Alignment Verification

C/B·01

After every pull, each datum point on the unibody is re-measured and compared against the factory specifications: length, width, and height. Suspension pickup points, rail heights, and strut towers must all land inside specification before we sign off the structure. You receive the measurement printout with your repair file.

Datum Verification: Factory Specifications · Printout Included

ADAS Sensor Recalibration

C/B·02

Automatic emergency braking, lane keeping, and adaptive cruise aim through radar and camera units that move when structure moves. Every structural repair concludes with the OEM calibration protocol: static targets, dynamic road calibration, and radar aim verification as the manufacturer specifies. A system with no warning light can still be seeing the road wrong, so every one gets calibrated.

Cal Protocol: Static / Dynamic · Radar Aim · Camera Targets

Printed Calibration Report

C/B·03

Delivery includes documented proof: structural measurements, calibration pass reports, and OEM parts invoices. It protects your resale value, supports any diminished value claim, and backs the lifetime warranty we put on the work, in writing, for as long as you own the vehicle.

Deliverable: Full Repair File · Lifetime Warranty Record
